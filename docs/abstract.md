# Abstract

*Editable short abstract (~150–200 words). Synced briefly into `paper/inflation_tax_canada.tex`.*

Edward N. Wolff introduced the net inflation gain identity \(NIG = IG - IT\), pairing the inflation tax on income with balance-sheet gains from debt devaluation and asset revaluation (NBER Working Papers 31775 and 29392). This paper adapts that accounting framework to Canada using the Survey of Financial Security (SFS, 1999–2023), with a complementary Survey of Household Spending (SHS) appendix for recent consumption incidence. We construct Wolff-style marketable net worth, consolidate registered and mutual-fund holdings into equity and fixed-income buckets (Parliamentary Budget Officer look-through for registered accounts; 60/40 for mutual funds), and compute \(NIG\) by wealth quintile, tenure, age, and analytic region. Middle Canadian wealth quintiles typically record positive \(NIG\) relative to income—qualitatively echoing Wolff’s U.S. conclusion that inflation is “not a tax” for the middle class—while the bottom quintile is more exposed to the income tax on thin balance sheets. Unlike the U.S. Survey of Consumer Finances, the SFS cannot support reliable inference about the ultra-wealthy. Tenure patterns diverge from a pure leverage narrative: free-and-clear owners show higher \(NIG\)/income than mortgagors. Strong life-cycle and geographic gradients appear; the SHS appendix documents stock–flow divergence between balance-sheet \(NIG\) and necessities shares of consumption.
